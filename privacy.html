Privacy Policy

Last updated: 5 November 2026 · Deutsche Fassung

This policy explains how we handle data in the apps published by Tickwork (Ines Golub) and on this website. Each individual app also carries a privacy label on its App Store page listing the data that specific app uses.

Controller

Ines Golub (trading as Tickwork), Germany. Contact: inesgolubapps@gmail.com. Full postal details are listed in the Impressum.

Our apps

Data we collect ourselves

Our apps do not require an account and we do not operate servers that store your personal data. Game progress, settings and similar app data stay on your device and, if you have enabled it, in your own iCloud backup — we have no access to either.

The exception is Centri Drift: the game offers online leaderboards, events and a backup of your progress, and stores data with Google Firebase for this. What exactly, who can see it and how to delete it is set out in its own Centri Drift privacy policy, which takes precedence over this policy for that game.

iCloud sync. Some apps — Glowward, for instance — also keep your progress in step across your own devices through iCloud (iCloud key-value storage). Only the game progress is synced (in Glowward: which puzzles you have solved, the colours you chose and which chapters are unlocked), together with a random identifier per device so that your devices can tell their entries apart. This data lies in your own iCloud account and is processed by Apple under Apple's privacy policy; neither we nor any advertising provider can access it. Without an iCloud account, progress simply stays on the device.

Camera, microphone and photos

Some apps can use the camera, the microphone or your photo library — Vortis, for instance, lets the flow respond to music in the room and can turn a camera image or a photo into its colour source. All of it is analysed on your device and nowhere else. Neither sound nor image is recorded, stored, or transmitted to us or to anyone else; the microphone signal is analysed in real time and discarded immediately, and the camera image is folded frame by frame into what you see.

To pick a photo we use Apple's system picker. It runs outside the app: we receive only the image you chose yourself and never see your library. If an app records your session, that recording is produced entirely on the device and saved to your own photo library — it goes nowhere else.

Each of these needs your consent in the system dialog and can be withdrawn at any time in the iOS settings. Without consent the feature in question is simply unavailable; the rest of the app keeps working. The legal basis is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).

Advertising

Some of our apps are financed through advertising. For this we use LevelPlay — the mediation platform formerly known as ironSource, now part of Unity Technologies. It passes ad requests on to connected advertising networks: Unity Ads in all of these apps, and in Glowward additionally AppLovin and Google AdMob. In this process, the following may be processed by these providers: a device identifier (on iOS the Identifier for Advertisers, IDFA, if you have consented — otherwise an app-scoped identifier assigned by the SDK), your IP address, coarse location derived from it, device and operating system information, and data about which ads were shown, viewed, clicked or led to an install.

Apps without a consent dialog: no personalised advertising in the EU. Apart from Glowward (see below), our apps do not use a consent management platform, and they therefore permanently signal to the advertising SDK that no consent for consent-based processing is available. Within the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom you consequently receive non-personalised advertising only in these apps: the ad shown is not selected on the basis of an interest profile built across apps and websites. The data listed above is still processed for technical delivery, billing the advertiser and fraud detection.

Glowward: consent dialog. Where the law requires consent — in particular in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom — Glowward asks for it with Google's User Messaging Platform (UMP), a consent management platform that works with the IAB Europe Transparency & Consent Framework (TCF). In the dialog you decide, per purpose and per provider, whether information may be stored on and read from your device and whether ads may be personalised and measured. Your choice is saved on your device as a TCF consent string; LevelPlay reads it and passes it on to the advertising networks. If you decline, ads still appear, but they are not personalised on the basis of your data, and Glowward does not ask the iOS tracking question either. If no answer is available — for example because the dialog could not load without a connection — Glowward signals to the advertising SDK that no consent has been given. You can review or change your choice at any time in Glowward under Settings → Privacy Settings; the new choice applies from the next app launch at the latest. To show the dialog and store your choice, Google processes your IP address (to determine your region) and technical information about your device and the app. Google acts here only as the provider of the consent tool; Glowward contains no Google advertising SDK.

Glowward: when it asks. Glowward asks nothing at first launch. Only once you have solved puzzle 16 of the campaign does it ask, in a calm moment after a solved puzzle: first the consent dialog (where required), then a short explanation of our own, then Apple's tracking dialog. The advertising and consent SDKs are not started before that point, and the advertising SDK only starts once you have answered. If you buy “Remove ads”, none of this happens: no dialog, no tracking question, and the advertising SDK is never started.

App Tracking Transparency. On iOS, access to the IDFA requires your explicit consent through Apple's ATT dialog. If you decline, no IDFA is read; advertising still appears, simply without that identifier. You can change this decision at any time under Settings → Privacy & Security → Tracking.

SKAdNetwork. We also use Apple's SKAdNetwork. It reports to an advertising network, in aggregated and time-delayed form, that an ad led to an install — without identifying you. It works without any device identifier and independently of your ATT decision.

Legal basis for delivering non-personalised advertising is our legitimate interest in financing the app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); where the IDFA is used, it is your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), given through the ATT dialog and revocable at any time. In Glowward, personalised advertising and the storing and reading of information on your device for advertising purposes are likewise based on your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR), given in the consent dialog and revocable at any time under Privacy Settings in the app; showing the dialog and recording your choice is based on our legal obligation to obtain consent and to be able to demonstrate it (Art. 6(1)(c) in conjunction with Art. 7(1) GDPR). Details on Unity's processing are set out in their Game Player and App User Privacy Policy, and details on the LevelPlay mediation layer in the ironSource privacy policy. For Glowward, AppLovin's processing is described in the AppLovin privacy policy, and Google's processing for the consent dialog in the Google privacy policy and on how Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Reports and e-mails to us

Some apps let you report something to us — in Lettern, for example, a missing or wrong word. The app collects such reports on your device only. They reach us only when you send them yourself by e-mail; the app shows you the finished message in your mail app first. We then receive the content of the e-mail (the reported words, the app and iOS version) and your sender address, use both only to handle the report and, where needed, to reply, and delete the e-mail once it is no longer needed for that. The legal basis is our legitimate interest in fixing errors in our apps (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

When an app opens a link — such as a dictionary entry on Wiktionary — Safari loads the page outside the app. The app itself transmits nothing; the privacy policy of the site's operator applies to that page.

Purchases

Purchases in our apps are processed by Apple through the App Store. We receive only aggregated, anonymised sales figures — never your payment details. Apple's own privacy policy applies to that transaction. To find out whether you own a purchase, the app asks the App Store on your device; that is also how a purchase is restored on your other devices or shared through Family Sharing where the app offers it. In Glowward, the one-time purchase “Remove ads” switches advertising off for good: from then on the advertising SDK is no longer started. While an advertising SDK is running in an app — before such a purchase, for example — it may register that an in-app purchase took place; the app's App Store privacy label lists this as purchase history.

Game Center

Some of our games submit a score — a result or a best time — to a leaderboard in Apple's Game Center. This happens only if you are signed in to Game Center on your device. The score is linked to your Game Center account and is processed by Apple under Apple's own privacy policy; we receive no personal data from it and see no more of you than the public nickname Game Center itself displays. If you are not signed in, the app keeps your best result on your device only and nothing is transmitted.

Crash reports and usage statistics

If you have enabled sharing analytics with developers in your iOS settings, Apple provides us with aggregated crash and usage reports. These do not identify you personally. Where an app uses an additional analytics or crash-reporting service, that is stated in the app's App Store privacy label.

Notifications

Some apps can remind you about something, such as the end of a running process. These notifications are scheduled and delivered entirely on your device — there is no push server, no device token and no transmission to us. Whether you allow them is your choice, made in the system dialog and changeable at any time in iOS Settings.

This website

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. When you visit it, GitHub processes technical connection data such as your IP address, browser type and the time of the request in server log files, for the purpose of delivering the site securely (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). We do not set cookies, run analytics or embed third-party trackers on this site. See GitHub's privacy statement for details.

Children

Our apps are not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal data from them. Accordingly, we signal to the advertising SDK that our apps are not child-directed within the meaning of the US COPPA. Should an app ever be intended for a younger audience, advertising will be configured differently and this will be stated in the app itself.

Data transfers outside the EU

The advertising, consent and hosting providers named above may process data in the United States and other countries outside the EU. These transfers are based on the EU standard contractual clauses or the EU–US Data Privacy Framework, depending on the provider.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right to access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability, and to object to processing based on legitimate interest. Where processing is based on consent, you may withdraw it at any time with effect for the future. To exercise any of these rights, write to inesgolubapps@gmail.com. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a data protection supervisory authority.

Because we do not store personal data ourselves, requests concerning advertising data usually need to be directed to the provider in question; we will help you identify the right contact.

Changes

We update this policy when our apps or the services they use change. The date at the top shows the current version.
